# Abstract

Cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) has advanced three-dimensional structure determination of macromolecular complexes. De novo modelling algorithms, including ModelAngelo and CryoAtom2, have automated the construction of protein and nucleic acid backbones. However, fully automated, accurate end-to-end reconstruction of ligands involved in catalysis, allosteric regulation and drug action remains challenging. Existing tools address individual steps, with inefficient connections between region localisation, chemical identity matching and pose reconstruction. Here we introduce LigandSeek, a fully automated end-to-end framework that jointly models density and receptor information through three stages: Find, Match and Build. On a benchmark of 179 complexes, Find achieved a one-to-one PRAUC@0.3 of 0.541, increasing to 0.663 with deposited receptor structures. Both exceeded the score of 0.255 achieved by the density-only method Emap2lig-Find. Match achieved 86.6% overall matching accuracy, with a mean matching time of 0.09 s per candidate region. It was substantially faster than EMERALD-ID and covered a broader range of ligands. For Build, we incorporated continuous cryo-EM density-field constraints into a receptor-based generative model and fine-tuned it. This reduced the median root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of top-1 small-molecule poses from 1.55 Å to 1.14 Å. The success rate for accurate poses (RMSD < 2 Å) increased from 62.9% to 78.0%. In end-to-end blind evaluation without deposited receptor structures as input, LigandSeek achieved success rates of 59.7% (RMSD < 2 Å) and 67.5% (RMSD < 3 Å) across 77 PDB entries. With deposited receptor structures, these rates increased to 66.2% and 71.4%, respectively. LigandSeek provides an efficient, fully automated workflow from continuous three-dimensional cryo-EM density maps to all-atom ligand poses.

# Introduction

Single-particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) has become an important method for determining the three-dimensional structures of macromolecular complexes[@callaway2020revolution; @bai2015cryo]. Deep learning tools such as ModelAngelo[@jamali2024modelangelo], CryoAtom2[@cryoatom2_2025] and EMProt[@emprot2024] have advanced automated modelling of protein and nucleic acid structures. Ligands bound in active pockets often control catalysis, conformational switching and signalling cascades in these complexes. Their three-dimensional conformations and binding poses also underpin structure-based drug design[@cheng2018cryo]. A key challenge is therefore to combine experimental density and the receptor environment to detect, identify and reconstruct accurate all-atom ligand structures automatically, without expert modelling.

From a computational biology perspective, automated end-to-end modelling of all-atom ligand complexes from cryo-EM density maps faces three interdependent bottlenecks.

The first is region localisation, the Find bottleneck. Cryo-EM density maps span large spatial scales, with heterogeneous local signal-to-noise ratios and diffuse, intermixed densities from water molecules or lipid micelles. Existing density detection methods, such as Emap2lig-Find[@umap2lig], mainly use three-dimensional voxel segmentation networks to locate small-molecule density blobs directly across the entire map. These density-only approaches omit information from the macromolecular receptor that coexists with the ligands. They lack the discriminative chemical and geometric context of the pocket, making it difficult to combine background exclusion with reliable instance-level localisation.

The second is chemical identity matching, the Match bottleneck. Once a ligand region has been located, it must be associated with a ligand chemical identity in the complex. Each identity is represented by a simplified molecular-input line-entry system (SMILES) string. EMERALD-ID[@emerald_id] docks candidate ligands individually and infers their identities from the resulting scores. Its computational complexity is $O(N_g \times K)$, where $N_g$ is the number of candidate sites and $K$ is the number of competing ligand identities. This computational cost, coupled with the inability to use GPU acceleration, can make ligand scoring at a single site take hours. Its primary scope is small-molecule ligands, limiting its use in systems containing multiple ligands.

Although Emap2lig connects region detection and pose reconstruction, it lacks automated identity matching and requires users to specify the correspondence between regions and ligand identities. The end-to-end process linking localisation, identity matching and pose reconstruction therefore remains incomplete[@umap2lig]. Rapid matching of candidate regions to chemical identities is thus essential for connecting localisation to reconstruction.

The final bottleneck is pose reconstruction, the Build bottleneck. After the binding region and chemical identity have been determined, generative docking models such as DiffDock[@corso2023diffdock] and PocketXMol[@pocketxmol] primarily constrain pose generation using receptor structures. They cannot use experimental density information to guide ligand pose generation. Conversely, density-only deep learning tools such as Emap2lig-Build[@umap2lig] do not explicitly model protein and nucleic acid receptors. They therefore struggle to satisfy both density fitting and receptor-environment constraints. Conventional methods without deep learning, including EMERALD, GemSpot and ChemEM, can combine the receptor environment and experimental density for pose search or optimisation[@muenks2023emerald; @robertson2020gemspot; @sweeney2024chemem]. However, they are slow and still require connections to upstream localisation and identity matching.

To connect localisation, matching and reconstruction, we developed LigandSeek, an automated end-to-end ligand modelling tool that jointly models density and receptor information ({{figref:fig-overview_v2,fig-method-v4}}). We jointly encode experimental density, receptor-derived simulated and difference densities, and the chemical and geometric environments of receptor atoms. These representations form the main shared inputs to the neural networks across all three stages.

LigandSeek follows a three-stage Find–Match–Build workflow:

1. LigandSeek-Find uses a hybrid point–voxel network to fuse continuous voxel-density representations with geometric and chemical information from the receptor point cloud. It detects three-dimensional ligand regions while balancing precision and recall.
2. LigandSeek-Match uses a cross-modal matching network with graph and context attention. It integrates ligand language representations, molecular graph representations and contextual information from other ligand identities in the same complex. This enables rapid, accurate identity matching for small molecules, metal ions and sugars.
3. LigandSeek-Build incorporates local density encoding into the generative docking model PocketXMol and fine-tunes it. Generated samples follow both experimental density contours and the chemical constraints of the receptor pocket, improving pose sampling accuracy.

We evaluated the framework on 179 non-redundant complexes comprising 2,502 ligand instances. Under blind evaluation conditions, only receptors modelled de novo by CryoAtom2 were used, with no deposited receptor structures provided as input. All three LigandSeek components outperformed their respective baselines. Find achieved a one-to-one PRAUC@0.3 of 0.541, compared with 0.255 for Emap2lig-Find. Match achieved 86.6% overall matching accuracy, with a mean matching time of 0.09 s per candidate region, substantially faster than conventional reverse docking. Build reduced the median root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of top-1 poses from 1.81 Å with the official model to 1.21 Å. The success rate at RMSD < 2 Å increased from 54.9% to 74.7%. In end-to-end blind evaluation across 77 complexes containing organic small molecules, the all-atom modelling success rate (RMSD < 2 Å) was 44.2% for a single attempt. Within the first ten attempts, the success rate was 59.7%. LigandSeek connects ligand localisation, identity matching and pose reconstruction to generate accurate all-atom ligand poses from cryo-EM density maps. It provides a new tool for drug discovery and high-throughput cryo-EM screening.
